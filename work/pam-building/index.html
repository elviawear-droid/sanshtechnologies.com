PAM Building

A drainage manufacturer’s commercial engine: special pricing agreements, director approvals, monthly merchant claims checked line by line, marketing funds and deal margins, in one system.

Client
PAM Apps, PAM Building
Scope
Commercial platform
Year
Since 2022
Stack
Vue, Python, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Azure Durable Functions, Docker, Azure App Service, GitHub Actions
pamapps.co.uk

The problem

PAM Building makes drainage and pipe systems. It sells through merchants, who sell on to the contractors doing the building work. To win a project, PAM agrees a special price for a contractor; the merchant sells at that price, and at the end of each month sends PAM a claim for the difference.

Those claims arrive as Excel files, each merchant in its own format, with the merchant’s product codes mixed in with PAM’s. Every line has to be matched to the right agreement, at the price that applied on the day of the sale, before anything is paid. Around that sit the agreements themselves, director approvals, marketing funds for merchants and the margin on every deal, much of which had lived in spreadsheets and email.

The approach

We built one system with three parts: a web app for the sales, admin, finance and director teams, an API that owns the data and every commercial rule, and a set of background functions for anything heavy or file-based. A claim file is handed to the functions and processed in the background, so a large file never holds up the app.

The rules that decide money are written once, in one place, and tested: how an agreement turns into a claimback, which version of an agreement applies to a sale, and how a margin is worked out. When an agreement changes, the old version is kept with its exact terms, so a late claim is always valued at the price that was agreed at the time.

An agreement, from deal to live

  1. 01Shape the dealThe calculator works out the merchant’s claimback from the list discount, rebate, merchant margin and contractor discount.
  2. 02Create the agreementMerchant, contractor, product ranges, dates and pricing terms, with extra ranges at their own prices where a deal needs them.
  3. 03Route for approvalDiscounts above the threshold for that product range and the submitter’s role go to a director before the agreement can go live.
  4. 04Go liveThe approved agreement is what every claim is checked against.
  5. 05Renew and keep historyEdits and renewals, one at a time or many at once, archive the previous version with its exact terms.

A claim, from Excel file to claimback

Every month each merchant sends a spreadsheet of what it sold at agreed prices. The claims engine turns it into a checked figure.

  1. 01Read the fileBackground functions recognise the merchant’s template and read every row, whatever the layout.
  2. 02Find the productPAM codes and the merchant’s own codes are told apart and resolved through cross references.
  3. 03Match the agreementEach line is matched to its agreement, including narrower product sub-ranges, using the version that was live on the date of sale.
  4. 04Check every linePrices are checked against the agreement, and lines that duplicate an earlier claim or fall outside the claim’s month are caught.
  5. 05Work out the claimbackAdjustments carry their reason, and the total owed is ready for review and approval.

What we built

What the sales team uses

Agreements and approvals

  • Special pricing agreements per merchant and contractor, with discounts, dates, ranges and pricing terms
  • Full version history, so every past claim is valued at the terms of its day
  • Edit or renew many agreements at once
  • Director approval for discounts above set thresholds
  • A claimback calculator for shaping deals
  • Microsoft sign-in and permission-based roles for each team

What turns a spreadsheet into a payment

Claims engine

  • Background processing of merchant Excel files with Azure Durable Functions
  • Templates for each merchant’s file format
  • Detection of PAM codes and merchant codes in the same file
  • Matching to agreements and product sub-ranges, at the prices of the day
  • Duplicate and out-of-period checks
  • Adjustments with a written reason on every changed line

What finance and the directors use

Commercial tools and data

  • Joint initiative funds: budgets per merchant across financial years, with carry-over
  • Fund requests raised, approved by a director and settled by finance, each with its own reference
  • Letters of authority and budget summaries generated as PDFs
  • A margin calculator showing the margin on every line of a deal, with loss-making lines flagged
  • Importers for products, prices, costs, cross references, stock, contacts, contractors, agreements and pricing terms
  • Every upload tracked, with processed and failed lines

In the app

How we run it

Checked against the real spreadsheet
The margin calculator is a direct transcription of the commercial team’s own workbook, checked against real deals they had priced, matching to two decimal places.
Imports you can run twice
Prices and costs are stored with the date they start from, so history is kept and uploading the same file again changes nothing.
Every release has a way back
Changes go to a staging copy first. Before each production release the database and the previous build are saved, and the release is checked against real figures once it is live.
Tested where the money is
Hundreds of automated tests across the API, the claims functions and the web app, including real deals, and every database change shipped as a migration.

The outcome

PAM’s commercial team runs agreements, approvals, claims, merchant funds and deal margins in one system instead of spreadsheets and email.

Claims are valued against the agreement that applied on the day of each sale, with every adjustment explained, and the margin calculator shows the loss-making lines a single deal total used to hide.

We keep extending it. Clearer claim errors by category, and re-checking a claim in place after a fix, are in testing now.
