Cast Iron Outlet

A UK supplier of cast iron rainwater and drainage products had a brochure website and took every order by hand. We built the shop, the back office and the API behind both, and we still run it.

The problem

Cast Iron Outlet sells cast iron rainwater and soil systems, roof outlets, and floor and shower drains. Its customers come in two kinds: homeowners who buy online, and trade buyers who expect a quote before they order. Every price carries VAT, and delivery depends on the postcode, with the Scottish islands and remote areas priced apart from the mainland.

When the work started, the website was a static brochure. It looked the part, but there was no basket, no checkout, no customer accounts and no order database. Every order was handled by hand, away from the site.

The approach

We split the platform into three parts that share one source of truth: a storefront for customers, an admin panel for staff, and an API that owns the database and every business rule. Both front ends are clients of the API, so a price, a delivery rule or an order status is defined once.

The rules that cost money when they are wrong (VAT, delivery pricing, discounts, quotes and payment status) live in the API, next to their tests. Work ships in small releases that go to a staging site first and reach production only from a separate release branch.

What we built

What customers use

Storefront

  • Catalogue with categories, search and filters that can be shared as a link
  • A basket that survives a reload, with the VAT breakdown and a live delivery price
  • Stripe checkout, customer accounts, saved addresses and order history
  • Trade quotes sent as links that open without logging in
  • A rainwater flow calculator that sizes gutters and downpipes from three questions about the roof
  • Server-rendered pages with structured data, a sitemap and ecommerce analytics

What staff use every day

Admin panel

  • Microsoft single sign-on for staff accounts
  • Products with families and variants, bulk updates and image upload
  • Orders with their payment status from Stripe, plus orders entered by hand
  • Quotes with custom pricing, delivery and stock
  • Discounts, sale pages, banners and menu promotions the team runs themselves
  • Customers and a sales lead pipeline

The engine underneath

API

  • Delivery pricing by postcode, including the Scottish islands and remote areas
  • Dispatch and delivery dates that respect the daily cut-off, weekends and UK bank holidays
  • Payments through Stripe, with order status kept in step by webhooks
  • Transactional email from responsive templates, with marketing consent recorded
  • Images converted to WebP and resized on upload
  • Customer accounts that lock after repeated failed logins

On the live site

How we run it

Schema changes as migrations
Every database change is a versioned migration that runs before the API starts. Nothing creates tables at runtime, so staging and production cannot drift apart.
Tests where the money is
The API has automated tests for delivery rates, quotes, sign-in, customer records and marketing consent, the places where a quiet mistake would reach a customer or an invoice.
Staging before production
Every change deploys to a staging site first. Production is a separate branch, so nothing reaches customers until it has been checked there.
Each change written up
Every feature and fix comes with a plain-English note of what changed and how to use it, so the client team can run new tools without waiting for us.

The outcome

The business now takes orders, payments and trade quotes online. Staff run the catalogue, orders, quotes and seasonal sales from the admin panel themselves.

We still look after the platform and keep adding to it in small releases.
