Mobile

Apps installed on phones and tablets, built for touch, sensors and use on the move.

In plain words

A mobile app is downloaded from the App Store or Google Play and runs directly on the device. That gives it access to things a website struggles with: the camera, location, push notifications and working offline.

Apps can be native, built separately for iOS and Android, or cross-platform, with one codebase for both using tools such as React Native or Flutter. Native gives the most polish; cross-platform usually costs less to build and maintain.

How it works

  1. 01InstallThe user downloads the app from an app store.
  2. 02OpenThe app starts on the device and shows its own screens.
  3. 03Work offlineData is kept on the phone so the app still works without signal.
  4. 04SyncWhen online, the app exchanges data with the API.
  5. 05UpdateNew versions pass app store review, then reach users.

Key ideas

Native or cross-platform
Two separate apps in Swift and Kotlin, or one shared codebase. The right choice depends on budget and how much the app relies on the device.
Offline first
Designing the app to work without a connection and sync later.
Push notifications
Messages the app can show even when it is closed, with the user’s permission.
App store review
Apple and Google check every release, so a fix can take a day or more to reach users.
