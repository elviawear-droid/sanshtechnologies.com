Services

The code that does the real work: the business rules behind every screen.

In plain words

Services hold the logic of a product. Placing an order, working out a price, checking stock and sending a confirmation are all jobs for services. The API passes requests in, and the services decide what should happen.

Small products usually keep all of this in one application, called a monolith. Larger ones may split it into separate services that can be changed and scaled on their own. Neither is always right, and most systems start as a monolith.

How it works

  1. 01ReceiveA request arrives from the API.
  2. 02Apply rulesBusiness logic decides what should happen.
  3. 03Read and writeThe service loads and saves the data it needs.
  4. 04AnnounceOther parts are told what changed, such as “order placed”.
  5. 05ReplyThe result goes back through the API to the app.

Key ideas

Monolith
One application that contains all the business logic. Simple to build, test and deploy.
Microservices
Many small services, each owning one part of the business and deployed on its own.
Domain
The area of the business a service is responsible for, such as billing or shipping.
Events
Services announce what happened instead of calling each other directly, which keeps them loosely connected.
